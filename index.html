    Rules and models

    Every question is answered twice — once by a hand-written rule, once by a regression trained only on bets already settled. The rule is the control, and it is never retired however badly it does. Judged on shared days only: the model may not bet until it has 25 settled examples, and counting the days it sat out would flatter whoever drew the easier ones.

    Brier decides it, not accuracy — accuracy cannot tell the difference between a cautious miss and a confident one.

    Calibration

    The one figure that cannot be rushed. Each mark is a confidence band: how sure it claimed to be, against how often it turned out right. On the dashed line means honest. Below it means bluffing. This needs months of evidence before it means anything, which is the whole reason the register is kept daily.

    Why this is hard to fake

    observe

    Six keyless public sources — crypto, geomagnetic storms, earthquakes, Wikipedia, Hacker News, the weather — read fresh at every run.

    expect

    Each rule commits to one binary call at a stated confidence, using only what was knowable at that moment.

    seal

    The entry is pushed before the outcome exists. The commit timestamp sits in public history and cannot be moved afterwards.

    settle

    Tomorrow's ordinary reading of the same source answers today's question. There is no separate oracle.

    audit

    The scorecard is re-derived from the archive on every run, so it is a pure function of the record — and verify.py will reject a run whose numbers do not reconcile.
